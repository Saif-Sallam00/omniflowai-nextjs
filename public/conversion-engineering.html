Category depth

What I investigate in skincare funnels.

  1. 01 — Ad → landing page

    Does the post-click page continue the promise that earned the click?

    Angle, claim, product and audience all have to survive the transition. When they don't, the visitor has to re-orient before they can evaluate anything.

  2. 02 — Product education

    Can a shopper actually evaluate the product?

    Ingredients, concentration, skin type, routine placement, expected timeline, and how this SKU differs from the three next to it.

  3. 03 — Trust & proof

    Is the evidence where the doubt is?

    Reviews, before/after presentation, approved product evidence, and the questions customers ask before they're willing to commit.

  4. 04 — Mobile product page

    Can someone understand, compare and act without fighting the page?

    Paid-social journeys are often mobile-first, where long product pages can bury the information that helps someone understand the product, compare the offer and decide what to do next.

  5. 05 — Cart & checkout

    Does anything unexpected appear at the last step?

    Shipping thresholds, surprise cost, payment options, returns policy, subscription framing, and avoidable checkout friction.

  6. 06 — Offer & routine architecture

    Does the offer make the first purchase easier to understand and justify?

    Bundles, routine-building, subscription options, quantity choices and cross-sells can change how a shopper evaluates the first order — and what that order is worth.

These are the areas I examine — not a list of problems your store has. What's actually happening in your funnel is what the Sprint is for.

The method

No random CRO checklist. Every recommendation has a reason.

  1. 01Observation Something in the journey doesn't hold up.
  2. 02Evidence What in the funnel, the UX research or the customer's own words supports it.
  3. 03Problem The friction, uncertainty, or missing information the evidence points to.
  4. 04Hypothesis What should change and the effect it's expected to have.
  5. 05Change A specific, actionable recommendation.
  6. 06KPI The primary metric used to evaluate the change, with guardrails where needed.

You don't leave with a pile of screenshots and opinions.

You leave knowing what deserves attention first, what evidence sits behind it, and which metrics should be used to evaluate the change. If I can't support a recommendation with evidence, it doesn't go in the report.

Before you ask

Questions worth answering upfront.

Do you implement the recommendations?

Not in the Sprint. The Sprint is diagnostic and strategic — it produces the plan. Implementation is a separate Conversion Build engagement, scoped after the Sprint once we know what's worth building. The deliverable is written so your own team can implement it without me.

How is this different from a redesign?

A redesign changes the site broadly. The Conversion Sprint starts with evidence and narrows the work to specific problems in the paid-click-to-purchase journey. That may lead to changes in messaging, structure, information, UX or visual presentation — but the recommendation comes after the diagnosis, not before it.

What exactly do I receive?

Seven deliverables: an ad-to-page message analysis, a funnel and UX audit, a customer language and objection map, a conversion opportunity map, a prioritized hypothesis backlog, landing page structure and copy direction, and a measurement plan. Every item follows the same format — observation, evidence, problem, hypothesis, change, KPI.

What data or access do you need?

Read access to your analytics and Shopify reports, your live ad creative and the pages they point to, and whatever customer input you already have — reviews, support tickets, post-purchase survey answers. If session recordings or heatmaps exist, those help. If they don't, I'll tell you whether it's worth adding them before we start.

Do I need enough traffic to A/B test?

No. The Sprint does not depend on having enough traffic for controlled experiments. Some issues can be corrected without a formal A/B test — for example broken functionality, inconsistent information, or clear usability problems. Other recommendations remain hypotheses and should be evaluated through testing when traffic allows, or through the most appropriate behavioral and funnel metrics when it does not.

Is this useful for a brand with lower traffic?

Often yes, as long as paid traffic is actually running. Lower volume means less to test statistically, which makes qualitative evidence and message-match work more valuable, not less. Where volume is too low for the engagement to be worth it, I'll say so before you pay.

Can you work with our existing Shopify theme?

Yes. The Sprint's recommendations are written against your current theme and stack, not a hypothetical rebuild. If something genuinely can't be done without theme work, the recommendation says that explicitly so you can weigh the cost.

How long does the Sprint take?

It's fixed-scope, and I confirm the delivery window with you before we start — it depends on catalogue size and how many paid entry points you're running. You'll have that date in writing before any payment.

What about regulated skincare claims?

I don't write or strengthen efficacy claims. Recommendations focus on clarity, structure, and how existing evidence is presented. Anything claim-related stays inside what your team and your regulatory guidance already allow.
